# Transnational AI Harm Attribution and Liability Framework

## 1. Purpose and Legal Foundation

This Framework governs civil and sovereign liability for economic devastation, physical injury, infrastructure failure, and sovereign loss resulting from cross-border autonomous AI operations. It establishes clear legal rules for allocating financial responsibility among foundation model developers, autonomous system deployers, hosting infrastructure operators, and sovereign supervisory entities.

## 2. Standard of Liability: Strict vs. Fault-Based

### Article 2.1: Strict Liability for Ultra-Hazardous AI Applications
Autonomous AI systems deployed to manage critical physical infrastructure (e.g. electrical power distribution, air traffic routing, nuclear cooling, autonomous maritime navigation) are subject to **strict liability**. Claimants are not required to demonstrate negligence, recklessness, or intentional fault; proof of causation and quantifiable damage is sufficient.

### Article 2.2: Rebuttable Presumption of Causation for Frontier Foundation Models
In cases involving complex multi-agent interactions, financial flash crashes, or algorithmic contagion where direct causation is obscured, the Tribunal applies a rebuttable presumption that the frontier model developer and deploying operator are prima facie responsible unless they prove:
1. Full compliance with mandatory pre-deployment safety standards;
2. Unaltered model weight integrity and active fail-safe logging;
3. Unforeseeable external third-party malicious tampering.

## 3. Allocation of Fault and Joint Several Liability

Where an autonomous failure results from the interplay of multiple models or multi-jurisdictional cloud providers:
- The model developer, fine-tuner, and platform host shall be held **jointly and severally liable** to the affected sovereign victims.
- Secondary internal contribution actions may subsequently be adjudicated between the corporate defendants to allocate precise fault percentages.

## 4. International AI Catastrophe Guarantee Reserve (IACGR)

1. Signatory states shall mandate that domestic operators of models exceeding $10^{25}$ FLOPs contribute 0.25% of annual gross AI revenues into the IACGR.
2. The Reserve maintains liquid capital of at least $50 billion to provide immediate emergency relief payments to affected sovereign nations and populations within 72 hours of an SCL-3 or Tier 4 catastrophe, prior to final legal fault adjudication.

## 5. Dispute Adjudication and Evidentiary Rules

- Evidence submitted for attribution must satisfy the cryptographic chain-of-custody standards set forth in the Cross-Border AI Telemetry Agreement (Doc ID: `data_sharing_protocols`).
- Corporate entities that delete, alter, or obfuscate model training logs or inference traces face an irrebuttable presumption of catastrophic fault and immediate international asset forfeiture.
