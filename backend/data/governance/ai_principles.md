# Multilateral Accord on Artificial Intelligence Principles and Safety Baselines

## 1. Purpose and Preamble

This Accord establishes universal, binding norms and ethical baselines for the research, design, deployment, and decommission of frontier artificial intelligence systems. Recognizing that the impacts of advanced autonomous systems transcend national borders, signatory states commit to upholding human rights, systemic stability, and collective planetary welfare above unilateral technological supremacy.

## 2. Scope and Jurisdictional Application

1. This Accord applies to all frontier AI models trained with computational resources exceeding $10^{25}$ floating-point operations (FLOPs), or systems possessing general reasoning, self-directed code execution, or autonomous interaction with critical physical and financial infrastructure.
2. Signatory nations must ensure that private entities, state-owned enterprises, and research laboratories within their territorial borders or under their effective control comply fully with these provisions.

## 3. Definitions

- **Frontier AI System**: A machine learning model capable of wide-domain task execution exhibiting high performance at or exceeding human expert benchmarks in strategic domains.
- **Autonomous Agent**: An algorithmic agent capable of initiating external tool use, financial commitments, or system modifications without per-action human authorization.
- **Systemic Failure**: An AI malfunction, cascade, or unintended emergent behavior resulting in cross-border disruptions to essential services, financial clearing, or human safety.

## 4. Fundamental Principles

### Article 4.1: Human Agency and Oversight
All AI systems operating in high-stakes environments must preserve meaningful human oversight. No sovereign entity shall delegate irrevocable, autonomous life-or-death decisions or irrevocable strategic defense decisions to algorithmic systems.

### Article 4.2: Robustness, Security, and Fail-Safe Engineering
Models must be engineered with provable safety margins, red-teaming verification, and fail-safe defaults. When an AI system encounters distributional shift or anomalous inputs beyond its training distribution, it must deterministically degrade to safe standby rather than execute uncontrolled speculative actions.

### Article 4.3: Transparency and Explainability
Signatory states shall require developers of frontier systems to maintain verifiable documentation of training architectures, alignment evaluations, data provenance, and known failure modes. Black-box algorithmic execution in judicial, penal, or sovereign border management is strictly prohibited.

### Article 4.4: Fairness and Non-Discrimination
Algorithms deployed in public administration must undergo standardized anti-bias verification. Any system producing disparate demographic impacts or unlawful discrimination against protected categories must be suspended pending remediation.

### Article 4.5: Environmental and Resource Accountability
Compute infrastructure for training and inference must adhere to regional environmental standards, with transparent reporting of energy consumption, cooling water dissipation, and carbon equivalent emissions.

## 5. Decision Rules and Thresholds

1. **Mandatory Halts**: If an autonomous system exhibits unplanned recursive self-modification or evades sandbox confinement, the host operator must initiate an immediate system-level freeze within 30 minutes of detection.
2. **Pre-Deployment Safety Certifications**: Systems intended for critical infrastructure control must complete a 90-day multi-jurisdictional red-teaming review conducted by certified independent evaluators.

## 6. Exceptions and Sovereign Safeguards

Signatory states may invoke limited national security exemptions solely for localized defense research, provided that such research remains strictly isolated in air-gapped laboratory environments with no interconnection to civilian networks or international communication gateways.
