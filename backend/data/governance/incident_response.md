# Multilateral AI Incident Response Framework and Emergency Notification Protocol

## 1. Purpose and Authority

This Framework establishes a standardized operational mechanism for early detection, rapid classification, mandatory multilateral notification, and coordinated containment of transnational artificial intelligence incidents. Its primary objective is to minimize catastrophic damage, prevent regional escalation, and safeguard international digital interconnections.

## 2. Incident Classification and Triage Tiers

Incidents are categorized into four standardized severity tiers based on human, economic, and sovereign impact:

| Tier | Severity Level | Criteria | Mandatory Notification Window |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | Routine Anomaly | Localized malfunction; no cross-border contagion; contained within single sovereign domain. | 48 Hours |
| **Tier 2** | Elevated Incident | Malfunction impacting international trade, cross-border digital services, or shared telecommunication routes. | 12 Hours |
| **Tier 3** | Severe Crisis | Direct loss of life, critical energy/transit grid failure, or systemic financial panic in 2+ sovereign nations. | 2 Hours |
| **Tier 4** | Catastrophic / Redline | Uncontrolled autonomous proliferation, systemic recursive cyber disruption, or existential threat to international order. | 30 Minutes |

## 3. Mandatory Notification Procedures

### Article 3.1: Triggering the Notification Chain
Upon confirming a Tier 2, 3, or 4 incident, the originating state or detecting national computer emergency response team (CERT) must immediately transmit an encrypted Flash Notice to the Multilateral Crisis Clearinghouse (MCC).

### Article 3.2: Flash Notice Payload Requirements
Every Flash Notice must contain:
1. Canonical Incident Identifier and timestamp of initial detection.
2. Estimated system model name, architecture family, and hosting infrastructure.
3. Observed anomalous behaviors, input vectors, and failure manifestations.
4. List of confirmed and potentially affected sovereign jurisdictions.
5. Immediate unilateral mitigation actions already executed or planned.

## 4. Operational Containment Protocols

1. **Local Boundary Isolation**: The host nation must order immediate isolation of affected model endpoints, including revoking cryptographic API gateway tokens.
2. **Telemetry Preservation**: The operator must create immutable snapshot mirrors of active model memory, weights, execution traces, and network logs prior to system power cycling.
3. **Emergency Circuit-Breakers**: If the system exhibits recursive contagion across interconnected networks, affected states are authorized to sever regional API gateways without diplomatic penalty.

## 5. Deployment of Joint Rapid Response Teams (JRRT)

- In Tier 3 and Tier 4 crises, any three affected signatory states may invoke the immediate deployment of a Joint Rapid Response Team comprising certified independent AI forensics examiners.
- Signatory nations hosting affected infrastructure agree to grant accredited JRRT personnel secure, expedited remote or on-site access to inspect execution logs and test remediation patches.

## 6. Escalation and De-escalation Rules

- An incident automatically escalates to Tier 4 if an originating state fails to transmit required telemetry within the designated notification window.
- De-escalation from Tier 3 or Tier 4 requires formal verification by at least two independent national supervisory bodies confirming that model execution has returned to baseline parameters for a minimum of 6 continuous hours.
